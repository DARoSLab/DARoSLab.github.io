*"Towards Robotic Companions: Understanding Handler–Guide Dog Interactions for
Informed Guide Dog Robot Design"* received a **Best Paper Award at CHI 2024** — an
honor given to the top 1% of submissions.

The study drew on **23 visually impaired guide-dog handlers and 5 guide-dog
trainers** to work out what a guide-dog robot actually needs: the right balance
between robot autonomy and human control, at least a two-hour battery, multiple
camera orientations for obstacle detection, audio sensing for hazards, and an
understanding of contextual commands such as "sidewalk." As Prof. Kim puts it,
*"there are 40 years of study there, and none of these robots are actually used by
end users"* — this work is about closing that gap.

Led by **Hochul Hwang**, with co-authors Hee-Tae Jung, Nicholas Giudice, Joydeep
Biswas, and Sunghoon Ivan Lee.

Human feet use an articulated toe to extend push-off and manage balance. We study
how robots can use the same mechanism for agile, dynamic locomotion through two
custom platforms: **PresToe**, a humanoid robot with an actuated toe, and
**StaccaToe**, a single-legged hopping robot that mimics the human leg and toe.

PresToe uses co-actuation and non-collocation to create an efficient and capable
robotic leg. We are developing trajectory optimization, model-predictive control
(MPC), whole-body control, and reinforcement learning strategies to unlock its
dynamic locomotion capabilities.

StaccaToe applies the same design principles to a single-legged hopper. Its
actuated toe provides a platform for studying push-off, balance, and agile
maneuvers while comparing legged robot mechanics with the biomechanics of the
human foot.
